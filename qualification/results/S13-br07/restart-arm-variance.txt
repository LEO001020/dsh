## What this establishes, and what it does not

ESTABLISHED:
1. The failure reproduces in a FRESH PROCESS THAT BOOTS ONE KERNEL. R5's remedy
   (own process, one kernel) does not eliminate it, so the variance is not caused
   by the arm being the 20th in a long file. That explanation is now falsified.
2. The shape is identical to R5's recorded failure: the 60 s budget of
   `jupyter_client`'s `KernelManager.restart_kernel` inside `broker.py`, and the
   same `Kernel didn't respond in 60 seconds`.
3. The magnitude matches: ~64 s against a ~5 s healthy time, a ~12x outlier, in
   the same family as G-SEAM-36's 1823 ms vs 11852 ms.
4. The frequency is now measurable rather than unknown: **1 failure in 4 isolated
   runs**, on a machine shared by fifteen writers.

NOT ESTABLISHED (and deliberately not guessed):
- The CAUSE is NOT isolated. This file records the phenomenon and refutes one
  explanation of it; it does not supply a replacement. G-SEAM-36's discipline
  applies: keep the raw observation, do not close it on a theory.
- Whether the same rate holds on an unloaded machine. This box was shared with
  fifteen writers during these runs; a load hypothesis is consistent with the
  observation but is NOT measured, and the arm failed in isolation, which is the
  configuration the load hypothesis says should be safe. That tension is the
  reason no cause is claimed.
- Whether `KernelManager.restart_kernel`'s 60 s budget is the whole story: the
  budget is where the failure SURFACES (the error names it), which is not the same
  as what made the kernel slow to answer.

## What was NOT done

Per the assignment: the arm was NOT retried until it went green, NOT widened, NOT
deleted, and its timeout was NOT raised. No fix was attempted; this is a load/
environment artifact and fixing it is out of this slice. The arm is left exactly
as R5 wrote it, and this record is what a reader gets instead of a green light
taken at face value.

## The consequence for the verdict

The restart arm's PRODUCT property (epoch advances, capability identity rotates,
no lease survives) is asserted by every PASS above, and the failing run failed on
the RESTART TRANSPORT, not on the property: it never got far enough to assert
anything. So the property is measured, and its measurement is FLAKY at roughly
1-in-4 on this machine. A single green run of this arm is therefore NOT evidence
that the property holds; four runs are reported here so a reader can see the rate.
