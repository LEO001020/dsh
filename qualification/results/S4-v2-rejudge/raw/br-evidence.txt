WHAT EACH ORACLE CLAUSE IS MET BY:
  `settled`            -> "a completed call carries `settled`, and the durable ledger row
                           is STARTED then SETTLED". Both stamps present, and the
                           settlement carries a sha256 DIGEST of the delivered value
                           rather than the value itself. The subCallId is derived from
                           the OUTER call id, so a reader holding the Session's `ipython`
                           execution can find every exact call it authorised.
  `cancelled`          -> the in-flight arm: a call dispatched and in the registry when
                           the cell settles is recorded `cancelled`.
  `abandoned-unstarted`-> a call still QUEUED BEHIND a serial lease when the cell returns.
                           The serial queue is what makes this deterministic rather than
                           a race (the test's own comment records that an earlier version
                           without the in-cell wait was FLAKY, returning zero ledger rows).
  `handed-to-jobs` + job id -> "a handoff names its job id; with no handoff the call is
                           abandoned-unstarted".
  "Nothing continues silently" -> "a call still queued when the cell returns is NEVER
                           DISPATCHED, whatever its disposition", plus "a FAILED
                           disposition write is reported, not swallowed, and CLOSED still
                           happens".

packages/dsh-ipython/src/bridge.ts:635-660, `settleQueuedCalls`, whose two arms ARE the
oracle's own distinction. The code's own comment states why mapping either onto the
other would erase the difference:
    "The two arms are the oracle's own distinction. A host handoff that takes ownership
     produces `handed-to-jobs` WITH the job id; with no handoff, the call is
     `abandoned-unstarted`. Mapping either onto the other would erase the difference
     between 'someone else owns this now' and 'this was refused', which is exactly what
     the oracle asks a reader to be able to tell apart."
